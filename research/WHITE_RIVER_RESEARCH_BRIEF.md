# White River (Bull Shoals Tailwater) — research brief

*Compiled 25 Aug 2026 from web research. Scope is deliberately narrow: the ~6 river
miles between Gaston's and the Narrows, in early October and early April, fished from a
rental jon boat tied to the bank. This is the source-of-truth research document; it is
not a guide. Prescriptions belong downstream of this file.*

## Confidence conventions

Every non-obvious claim below carries one of these. Preserve them when this material is
restructured — the difference between a measured number and a forum anecdote matters a
lot on this river.

| Tag | Means |
|---|---|
| `[CONFIRMED]` | Primary source: USACE, AGFC, SWPA, USGS, or a regulation text |
| `[REPORTED]` | Consistent across multiple guides/outfitters/AGFC weekly reports |
| `[INFERRED]` | My reasoning from the above; not stated anywhere directly |
| `[CONFLICT]` | Sources disagree; both values recorded |

## 1. The situation this research is scoped to

Ground truth about how the water actually gets fished. Everything downstream should be
tested against these, because most published White River advice assumes a drift boat.

- **Base:** White Hole Resort, Flippin AR. Rental jon boat, prop.
- **Reach:** upstream limit Gaston's; downstream limit the island immediately below
  Cranor's White River Lodge — i.e. the Narrows. Wildcat Shoals is past the turnaround.
- **Method:** drive to a spot, **tie to the bank, fish from inside the boat.** Anchors
  only in very low water. **They do not drift** — this is a fixed preference, not a
  tactical choice, and it is the single most important constraint. Any published
  technique that assumes boat movement has to be re-derived.
- **Windows:** early October and early April. Two trips a year.
- **Bait available:** PowerBait, night crawlers/red worms, sculpin or minnows,
  corn/shrimp/eggs.
- **Budget constraint:** no new tackle. Prescriptions must resolve to owned gear
  (see §10) or to consumables under ~$20.

**The consequence that runs through everything:** a stationary boat in current is
closer to bank fishing than to drift fishing, and closer still to steelhead swing
fishing. Weight, leader length and presentation all have to be re-derived from that.

### The White Hole itself

**The "White Hole" is a deep hole in the river, not a place name.** White Hole Resort's
own copy: *"Located at the head of the river's deep 'White Hole'."* `[CONFIRMED]`

Two structural consequences, and these are the most actionable facts in the brief:

1. **The deep water starts at the ramp and runs downstream from it.**
2. There are **weed beds a short run upstream** of the ramp holding rising rainbows and
   browns; two independent trip reports describe fishing them successfully at **five
   units** of generation. `[REPORTED]` Weed beds are where the sowbugs and scuds live;
   trout sit on the edge and pick them off, at every flow.

### Bank sides `[INFERRED]`

The Marion/Baxter county line follows the river. Marion County addresses (MC 70xx —
Stetson's, White Hole, White River Trout Club, Wildcat Shoals Resort) are on the
right-descending bank; Baxter County addresses (Cranor's on CR 703, Wildcat Shoals
Access on Denton Ferry Rd) are on the left-descending bank. **Verify on satellite
before relying on it.**

### Gaps

- **No named island verified between Gaston's and Wildcat.** The AGFC tailwater map
  names a **"Cane Island"** but I could not place it. The Narrows island is
  well-attested but unnamed in the sources I reached.
- The AGFC map also names holes — **Rainbow Hole, Armstrong Hole, Rough Hole, Blue
  Hole, White Hole, Johnson Bar** — and shoals *Tucker, Three Chutes, Bruce Creek,
  Partee, Roundhouse*. **Only White Hole is placed.** Placing the rest would be a real
  upgrade to this brief; the source is
  `http://www.cottertroutdock.com/mobi_BullShoalsTailwaterMap.pdf`.
- No source documents **depth or bottom composition** spot-by-spot in this reach.

## 3. Hydrology

### 3.1 Generation mechanics

| Fact | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Units at Bull Shoals | **8** | `[CONFIRMED]` |
| Plant capability (SWPA operating) | **391 MW / 26,400 cfs max turbine discharge** | `[CONFIRMED]` |
| Per unit | ~49 MW, ~3,300 cfs | `[CONFIRMED]` |
| MW → units | divide by ~49 | `[CONFIRMED]` |
| MW → cfs | multiply by ~67.5 | `[INFERRED]` from 26,400/391 |
| Minimum flow, design | **800 cfs** = 590 turbine + 50 station service + 160 wicket-gate leakage | `[CONFIRMED]` USACE |
| Minimum flow, as reported | AGFC prints 700; two resorts print 850; Dally says 600 | `[CONFLICT]` — treat **700–850** as the real band |
| Minimum flow on the SWPA forecast | displays as **7 MW** | `[CONFIRMED]` |
| Max planned release | 32,500 cfs, constrained by 50,000 cfs at Batesville and target stages at Newport | `[CONFIRMED]` |

Units are **not binary** — each dials 0→max, so "3 full units" and "6 units at half" are
the same water. `[CONFIRMED]`

**Minimum flow history:** authorized 2006, FEIS 2009, storage agreement signed by AGFC
Jan 2012, SCADA work complete May 2013, storage capture from June 2013. Before it, "zero
generation" meant ~50 cfs. It cost 5 vertical feet of flood storage (conservation pool
654.0 → 659.0). **It is not guaranteed** — the FEIS states minimum-flow releases halt
when the allocated storage is spent and don't restart until inflows recharge it.
`[CONFIRMED]`

**Who decides what:** USACE Little Rock sets the *volume* from flood-control rules and
lake elevation; SWPA schedules the *shape* to serve electrical peak demand.
`[CONFIRMED]`

### 3.3 Travel time — the safety clock

Canonical angler table `[REPORTED]`, times measured from generation start at the dam:

| Point | RM | Rise arrives |
|---|---|---|
| Gaston's | 5 | ~45 min |
| White Hole | 8 | ~90 min |
| The Narrows | ~9–10 | ~2–2.5 hr `[INFERRED]` |
| Wildcat Shoals | 11 | ~3 hr |
| Cotter | 18 | ~4.5 hr |
| Calico Rock | 62 | ~24 hr |

⚠️ **This table is internally inconsistent** — 5 mi in 45 min is 6.7 mph; 11 mi in 3 hr
is 3.7 mph. The short-reach figures reflect the **arrival of the surge front**, which
outruns the bulk water; the long ones reflect water velocity. Both are useful; don't
average them. `[INFERRED]`

Water velocity by generation `[REPORTED]`, consistent across three outfitters:

| Units | Speed |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1.75–2.0 mph |
| 2 | 2.25–2.5 mph |
| 3–4 | 2.75–3.0 mph |
| 5–6 | 3.25–3.5 mph |
| 7–8 | 3.5–5.0+ mph |

Dally advises **assuming 4 mph as the safety margin** on a rise. `[REPORTED]`

**Fall-out rule:** distance in miles ÷ 2 = hours for ~85% of the water to drop out.
Gaston's ~2.5 hr, White Hole ~4 hr, the Narrows ~5 hr. `[REPORTED]`

**This is what makes the reach fishable:** you gain roughly 2 extra hours of low water
at the Narrows versus Gaston's. Run downstream through the morning to stay in low water,
work back upstream in the evening as it drops.

> **There is no downstream warning siren.** There's a horn at the dam and it is
> inaudible even at the state park. Cranor's states flatly that no warning system exists
> downstream of either dam. `[CONFIRMED]`

### 3.4 The level ladder

Tailwater stage anchors `[REPORTED]`, from AGFC weekly reports: 452.14 ft @ 1,868 cfs ·
452.26 ft @ 2,199 cfs · 455.57 ft @ ~10,879 cfs. Dally: *"On the White 452 is roughly
normal low water height."* → **~3.4 ft of stage change at the dam between ~2,000 and
~11,000 cfs.** An outfitter near Cotter reports ~11 ft of total river-level change
between 850 and 26,400 cfs. **No published stage–discharge rating was found.**

| Level | cfs | ~MW | Sinker, *tied* boat | Meaning for a rental jon boat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Min flow | 700–850 | 7 | 1/8–3/16 oz | Gravel bars exposed. Prop strikes are the risk. **White Hole ramp launching is a documented problem at low water.** Bank-tie easy, anchoring safe, wading wide open |
| 1 unit | ~3,300 | ~49 | 1/4 oz | Best all-round level. Channel runs cleanly, shoals passable with care |
| 2–3 units | 6,600–10,000 | ~100–150 | 3/8 oz | Fish move to banks and flooded grass. Anchor only with a plan — a bump to 4 units reaches Gaston's in 45 min |
| 4–5 units | 13,000–16,500 | ~200–250 | 1/2 oz | Boat water. **Drag chain, not anchor.** Bank-tie needs a real eddy. Big-brown window |
| 6–8 units | 20,000–26,400 | 300–391 | 3/4–1 oz | Fish slack water only. No wading. Debris. Not a small-rental proposition |

⚠️ **The sinker column is `[INFERRED]`.** Every published White River weight chart is a
**drift** chart, sized to tick bottom while the boat moves at current speed. A tied boat
has zero drift and must resist full velocity — roughly double. The two sourced anchors:
bank/stationary on the White is *"1/4 oz to 1/2 oz depending on current speed and
depth"*; anchored doctrine is *"enough weight to keep bait positioned just above the
bottom rather than dragging."* Corroborating scale: White Hole Resort's published **lure**
ladder is 1/16–1/6 oz at min flow, 1/6–1/4 at 1–3 generators, 1/4–3/8 at 4–8.

**On-water calibration:** cast quartering upstream, let it settle. It should hold, then
slip a few inches and re-grab when the tip is lifted. Never moves = over-weighted.
Never stops = under-weighted.

**Drag chains are legal on the White and banned on the Norfork.** `[REPORTED]`

### 3.5 Lake level and drawdown

Bull Shoals top-of-conservation ("seasonal power pool") `[REPORTED]`, with the 659/662/695
anchors independently `[CONFIRMED]`:

| Dates | Elevation (ft msl) |
|---|---|
| Jan 1 – Apr 30 | **659** |
| May 1 – May 15 | 660 → 662 |
| May 15 – Jun 15 | **662** |
| Jul 15 – Sep 30 | **661** |
| **Sep 30 – Oct 1** | **661 → 659** |
| Oct 1 – Dec 31 | **659** |

Bottom of power pool 628.5; top of flood control 695.0.

**Anything stored above the seasonal target for that date is flood storage and gets
evacuated as fast as downstream conditions allow.** Observed correspondence `[REPORTED]`:

| Lake elev vs pool | Typical release |
|---|---|
| Below pool (651–658) | Min flow + short peaking bursts, 0–4 units |
| At/just over (659–662) | 2–4 units, min-flow gaps remain |
| 4–8 ft over (663–667) | ~16,000 cfs round the clock, no wadable water |
| 12–15 ft over (671–674) | 24,000+ cfs, spillway gates joining the turbines |

**The seasonal asymmetry is the crux `[INFERRED]`:** the fall drawdown is only 2 ft
(661→659 on Oct 1) and lands when inflows have collapsed — a few days of moderate
generation, not a month. Spring has **no scheduled drawdown at all**; the target *rises*
May 1–15. Spring high water is runoff filling the flood pool and the Corps' obligation
to get it back out.

> **Single best predictor for either trip: Bull Shoals lake elevation against 659 ft,
> checked the week before.**

**Current state (25 Aug 2026):** pool **657.86 ft** — below the 661 summer target and
slightly below the 659 October target. Basin has been dry since 2025 (Feb 2026: 653.09;
April 2026 Calico Rock 3,100–6,530 cfs). **If it holds, October 2026 should be
minimum-flow-heavy.**

## 4. Seasonality: early October vs early April

### 4.1 The hard numbers

USGS 07060500 (Calico Rock, 1943–2026) daily statistics. Includes Norfork + Buffalo +
Crooked Creek, so use the **ratio**, not the absolute.

| Day | April median (P25–P75) | October median (P25–P75) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 11,700 (6,480–21,200) | 4,940 (2,440–8,720) |
| 5 | 14,600 (5,750–23,400) | 5,000 (2,620–8,890) |
| 10 | 14,300 (6,870–22,600) | 4,410 (2,170–7,040) |

**Early April runs ~2.5–3× early October at the median. April's 25th percentile roughly
equals October's median.** April was the higher month in **12 of 14 years** (2012–2025);
April median ≈ 17,000, October median ≈ 6,000.

Dated snapshots at the dam `[REPORTED]`:

- **Oct 5 2023** — lake 3 ft below pool. *"Minimum flows in the morning followed by much
  higher releases averaging 4–5 units (12–15,000 cfs) in the afternoon."*
- **Oct 10 2024** — lake 651.71 (8 ft below pool). Min flow early morning, up to four
  units evening, back to min by next morning.
- **Oct 9 2025** — lake 658. Generation *"up to four hours daily."*
- **Mar 28 2023** — lake 665.35 (6.35 over). *"16,000 cfs around the clock."*
- **Apr 13 2023** — lake 673.09 (14 over). **24,000+ cfs, ~7 units plus spillway.** *"The
  White has had no wadable water."*
- **Feb 20 2026** — lake 653.09. *"Mostly at minimum flow of 700 cfs, with occasional
  rises to three units."*

### 4.2 Daily and weekly shape

Actual SWPA schedules, Bull Shoals, Aug 2026 `[CONFIRMED]`:

| Day | Profile (MW, hour-ending) | Daily total |
|---|---|---|
| Wed 12 Aug | 7 ×11 hrs, then 35, 107, 207, 307, 307, 307, **320**, 307, 207, 107, 25, then 7, 7 | 2,299 MW |
| Sat 22 Aug | 7 ×11, 35, 7, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 40, then 7 ×6 | 736 MW |
| Sun 23 Aug | 7 all day except 100, 100 at hrs 17–18 | 354 MW |

**Saturday's whole-day energy was 32% of the weekday's; Sunday's was 15%. Weekends are
reliably lower.** Sunday morning is the highest-probability low-water slot of the week.
`[INFERRED]`

⚠️ The Saturday 0 MW rows at hours 14–18 sit *below* the 7 MW minimum-flow marker.
Unexplained — possibly a scheduling artifact. Worth a call if it recurs.

Summer pattern is min flow overnight, ramp at 8–9 am for air conditioning. **October
inverts it** — min flow through the morning, ramp in the afternoon/evening, because it's
a shoulder month with neither cooling nor heating load. `[REPORTED]`

### 4.3 Verdict

**Early October is materially the better window.** Median flow ~⅓ of April; the
documented pattern is minimum flow dawn through mid-morning with generation arriving
afternoon/evening. From a noon start the rise reaches Gaston's ~12:45, White Hole
~1:30–2:00, the Narrows ~2:30–3:00 — a full low-water morning **plus** a fishable rise.

**Early April is a coin flip weighted toward high water.** In wet years the lake is over
pool and the Corps runs 16,000–24,000+ cfs continuously with no morning window at all.
In dry years it looks like October. Plan April around the boat; treat wading as a bonus.

## 5. Fish and forage

### 5.1 Temperature

| Reading | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 8 2026 | 51 °F | AGFC |
| Mar 12 2026 | 50–54 °F | AGFC |
| Aug 12 2026 | 12.5 °C = 54.5 °F | USGS 07054501 |
| Aug 25 2026 | 12.9 °C = 55.2 °F | USGS 07054501 |

Published characterizations range 48–54 °F, drawn from ~120 ft down. Annual band a few
miles below the dam is roughly **47–56 °F**. `[CONFIRMED]`

- **Early October ≈ 53–56 °F at the dam** — the annual *maximum* or within a degree.
  `[INFERRED]`
- **Early April ≈ 48–52 °F at the dam** — near the annual minimum. `[INFERRED]`

**Gain with distance is large and flow-dependent.** On 16 Aug 2026 the dam ran ~55 °F
while Calico Rock (~60 mi) was 66.2 °F. At minimum flow in October, expect **+3 to +6 °F
by the Narrows** on a warm afternoon; in April, +1 to +3. Under 2+ units the whole reach
is essentially dam temperature. `[INFERRED]`

Trout feed and grow best ~54–63 °F. **Early October is the warmest, highest-metabolism
window in which this river is comfortably fishable** — fish will chase. April at 48–52 °F
favors slow, deep, dead-drifted presentations and the warmest hours.

⚠️ **October DO caveat:** reservoir hypolimnetic DO bottoms out late Aug through Sep.
Bull Shoals runs turbine venting to compensate (SWPA floor target 4 mg/L; readings at the
dam were 8.2–8.5 mg/L in Aug 2026). Early October is still the low end of the DO year —
land fish fast, keep them wet. AGFC is explicitly asking for extra care given the
depleted population. `[CONFIRMED]`

### 5.2 Brown trout in early October — pre-spawn, not spawning

The hard citation: Quinn & Kwak (USGS/AFS) documented brown trout spawning in White
River, Arkansas tailwater reaches **from 11 October to 23 November 1996**, with juvenile
emergence beginning 28 February 1997. `[CONFIRMED]` AGFC's protective closures run
**Nov 1 – Jan 31**; AGFC weekly reports had browns "entering spawn season" on 30 Oct 2025
and "in full swing" by 8 Jan 2026. `[CONFIRMED]`

**Early October is genuinely pre-spawn/staging** — aggression without redds.

- Browns have shifted decisively to **piscivory**; USGS consumption work identifies this
  ontogenetic shift as why browns outgrow rainbows at the same sites. `[CONFIRMED]`
- **Sculpin here run 5–6 inches.** Don't fish small. `[REPORTED]`
- **Where they stage:** upper ends of holes (one guide found oversize browns across a
  150-yard stretch in **5 feet** of water); the drop-off at the tail of each shoal where
  gravel falls into the run; undercut banks, root wads, log jams, submerged timber; deep
  slots above and below gravel shoals. `[REPORTED]`

**Honest ranking:** November–January produces the biggest average fish, and
February–March (shad kill) the highest density of trophy opportunity — the record 38 lb
7 oz White River brown (Calvin Johnston, Dec 2019) came in a snowstorm at 17 °F, from
the bank, on a small trout-imitating lure. But early October offers: the top C&R mile
still open, annual peak water temperature, typically low flows, and no redds. Call it
the best *aggression-per-accessibility* window; December is the best raw-size window.

### 5.3 Rainbows in early April — and the hatchery situation

Rainbows here are effectively put-and-take with holdovers; AGFC states they must be
stocked annually because they don't reproduce meaningfully in Arkansas. **By early April
they're post-spawn and in normal feeding mode.** `[CONFIRMED]`

**The hatchery crisis materially changes the fishery:**

- **Oct 2025:** Norfork National Fish Hatchery (largest coldwater facility in the USFWS
  system, normally 3–3.5 M trout) suffered a water-quality die-off estimated at
  **80–90% of stock.** Jim Hinkle had already been cut to 50% capacity by April 2025
  flooding. `[CONFIRMED]`
- **Oct 16 2025:** AGFC emergency order — the whole 45-mile Bull Shoals tailwater to
  catch-and-release.
- **Feb 1 2026 → current:** see §9.
- **Aug 20 2026:** stockings still tiny and all **below Norfork** (2,100 rainbows
  Norfork–Chessmond Ferry; 1,400 Chessmond–Calico Rock). **Nothing in the dam-to-Wildcat
  reach.** AGFC: trout numbers "remain below historical averages."

**Tactical consequence, and it isn't all bad `[INFERRED]`:** lower catch rates on
generic PowerBait/spinner fishing; a higher proportion of holdovers and wild-behaving
fish eating natural food; more forage per predator, so better-conditioned browns. The
USGS work already flagged that reducing rainbow stocking would *improve* big-fish catch
rates. **Trophy odds are arguably better than normal; numbers odds are worse.**

### 5.4 Forage, ranked

| # | Item | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sowbugs** (aquatic isopods) | The signature food of these tailwaters. In the comparable Little Red tailwater, isopods were **70–80% of all prey items** in brown stomachs. No seasonality. Sizes 14–16 |
| 2 | **Scuds** (*Gammarus*) | Dislodged in numbers on a rise. Pink/cerise and natural brown |
| 3 | **Midges** | Year-round, *"far more plentiful than any other insect,"* 18–24 |
| 4 | **Sculpin** | 5–6 in. The engine of trophy brown growth. Peak relevance October pre-spawn and any low-light or high-water period |
| 5 | **Threadfin shad** entrained through the dam | **Shad kill runs Feb–March**; colder/longer cold snaps = bigger kill. Also pulled through any time generation is heavy — shad patterns still produce into early April on big spring water |
| 6 | **Crayfish** | Underrated. AGFC reports repeatedly name **"soft-shell crawdads"** as a top natural bait for browns. Most relevant in October |
| 7 | **Worms washed in on a rise** | A predictable pattern, not folklore. AGFC verbatim (Aug 2026): *"Cast live worms or San Juan Flies near banks as water rises from generation."* Top-three producer in the first hour of a rise, both seasons |
| 8 | **Caddis** larvae/pupae | Spring-dominant; green rock worms year-round in the shoals |
| 9 | Minnows / river shiners | Live redfin shiner on a 2/0 with a drop sinker is a documented big-brown method |
| 10 | Terrestrials | Hoppers/ants/beetles Jun–Sep, still viable on warm early-October afternoons. Browns reportedly favor **pink** and **black/purple** hoppers |
| 11 | Eggs | Peak Nov–Feb; yellow/peach with an orange bead produce year-round |
| — | Blackfly larvae | Present in fast shoal water, but **no Arkansas-specific source treats them as a targeted food item here.** Don't build on it |

**October order:** sculpin > crayfish > sowbug/scud > midge > worm-on-the-rise >
terrestrial > shad (opportunistic).
**April order:** caddis larva/pupa > sowbug/scud > midge > shad (if high water) >
worm-on-the-rise > streamer/sculpin > eggs (fading).

### 5.5 Hatches

**Early October — a nymph-and-streamer month.** Midges year-round 18–24. Published BWO
windows are Nov–Dec and Jan–Feb, so early October is normally *before* the fall BWO — a
cold cloudy front might catch the leading edge. White Fly (*Ephoron*) is listed Aug–Sep,
mostly finished. Terrestrials still in play. **Don't plan an October dry-fly trip.**

**Early April — the front edge of the caddis, usually not the peak.** Published windows
run March–May (spotted and cinnamon sedges, 12–18), but **Dally's (27 Mar 2025): *"The
true start to the caddis emergence has been sliding back in April."*** Two hard
conditions govern whether early April catches it:

1. **Flow.** It *"will truly fire when there is water flows of around 4000 or less."* In
   April 2025 the river ran 15,000 → 23,000 cfs and the hatch was sporadic.
2. **Direction.** The hatch normally **works upstream from the lower river** — in early
   April, water below Cotter and around Rim Shoals is typically ahead of the dam-to-
   Narrows reach.

**The tell:** evening swarms of egg-laying caddis, activity picking up **after 5 pm**.
Also in April: BWO on grey days, March Browns Apr–May, Sulphurs late Apr–May (high water
*prolongs* the sulphur hatch).

**Subsurface caddis pupa/wets are productive before, during and after the hatch** and
Dally calls it *"perhaps one of the best ways to find trophy brown trout."*

### 5.6 Holding water, low vs high

**At minimum flow** the river is an enormous spring creek. Big fish concentrate in the
deepest slots and troughs; the heads of holes where a shoal dumps in; the downstream lip
of every shoal; undercut banks, root wads, log jams, boulder pockets; and the seam
between the main tongue and the slack beside it. **The single most important low-water
feature is the seam where the main tongue runs past a moss/grass bed** — that's where
the scuds and sowbugs live. Big browns go largely nocturnal and crepuscular, and they
see you.

**On rising water** trout push to the banks and into newly flooded grass and gravel bars
that were dry an hour earlier — soft current, dislodged food. Big browns take station on
the bank edge, behind bank structure, on inside bends, and along the shelf-to-channel
drop-off. Fish seams, foam lines and defined runs in 4–5 ft **along the banks**, not open
water. Cloud, rain or wind chop on a bright day pulls larger trout shallow to hunt.

### 5.7 Timing

Mountain Home normals: October 71/48 °F, 3.81"; April 70/47 °F, 4.71". **Air temps are
nearly identical — the ~5 °F water difference is what matters.**

**The universal rule both seasons: fish the generation change.** The leading edge of a
rise and the first hour of falling water outproduce any clock time.

**Duration beats magnitude** `[REPORTED]` — Cotter Trout Dock: *"the river fishes much
better on the days they run water for 3 hours… When they only run for about an hour, the
bite tends to get pretty tough."*

- **October:** first light to ~10 am and the last two hours of daylight. Night is a
  legitimate play for pre-spawn browns.
- **April:** bite skews later — mid-morning through afternoon as things warm, then the
  evening caddis window after 5 pm. **Overcast and rainy days are the best days.**
  Watch Crooked Creek and the Buffalo: when they rise, the Corps *cuts* generation to
  protect downstream (Newport governs), which can hand you surprise low water.

### 5.8 Size expectations

USGS C&R-area densities: rainbows **47–342 fish/hectare**; browns **3–84/hectare**.
Rainbows were feeding at *submaintenance* levels in most seasons; browns grew well
across all size classes. Historic AGFC electrofishing (1993–94) put the Bull Shoals
tailwater at **188 brown trout per mile in excess of 5 lb.** `[CONFIRMED]`

Realistic competent DIY day: double-digit numbers, mostly rainbows 10–14", browns
commonly 14–20". One fish over 16" is a good day; over 20" a very good one. **Temper
rainbow expectations for 2026–27; brown expectations are intact or better.**

Records: White River brown 38 lb 7 oz (Dec 2019). Arkansas state brown 40 lb 4 oz (Rip
Collins, Little Red, 1992). State rainbow 19 lb 1 oz (1981).

## 6. Tackle and rigs

### 6.1 The White River rig

**It is not a Carolina rig and not a true three-way.** It is a **tied-in-line dropper
loop** — one continuous piece of leader split into a short weight leg and a long hook
leg. Local names: "White River rig," "White River drift rig," "Y rig." `[REPORTED]`

**Canonical build:**

1. 30–40 in of leader, **2–6 lb (4 lb standard)**
2. Dropper loop **6–10 in from one end**; cut one leg → the "Y"
3. Short leg (6–10 in) → **bell sinker**
4. Long leg (18–36 in) → **hook**
5. Top to main line via a **size 10–12 barrel or snap swivel**, or tie direct

Documented dimensional variants:

| Source | Sinker dropper | Hook leader | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| OzarkAnglers (KS-Rhino) | 6–10 in | 18–24 in | 24–30 in total, 2–4 lb, sometimes fluoro |
| Cotter Trout Dock | 10–18 in | 24–36 in | *"Tie a knot in the line to create a 'Y'"* |
| Carl Meeks (guide) | 8 in | 30–36 in total | 4 lb main, No. 6 hook, No. 10 bell |
| Donald Cranor (guide) | 6 in | 18 in | Bead + bare No. 6 VMC; 10 lb PowerPro main, 8–10 lb fluoro leader |
| OzarkAnglers (Hotdawg) | — | 3–4 ft | **No swivel at all** — *"the bigger fish don't get big by being dumb"* |
| OzarkAnglers ("Feathers and Fins") | 2 ft (three-way) | 2 ft | Sinker attached with a **rubber band** as a breakaway |

**Varying it:** faster water → *shorten* the hook leader (18–24 in), a long leader lays
flat downstream. Min flow → *lengthen* (30–48 in). Snaggy bottom → tie the sinker
dropper in **lighter line than the leader**, or use the rubber-band breakaway. Bigger or
spookier fish → drop the swivel entirely.

**Hook by bait** `[REPORTED]`:

| Bait | Hook |
|---|---|
| Power Eggs / PowerBait worms | #6 or #8 bait hook, or small circle |
| Night crawler (whole or stub) | #2 or #4 Aberdeen; guides also use #6 |
| Red worm | #4 Aberdeen |
| Sculpin / shrimp / crawdad | #1 or #2 Aberdeen (small fine-wire or circle for tiny sculpin) |
| Minnow | #6 Eagle Claw Kahle, through both lips |
| Corn / single egg | #10–#12 |

**Bell sinker numbering** (local shorthand is the number, not ounces): #10 = 1/8 oz,
#9 = 3/16, #8 = 1/4, #7 = 3/8, #6 = 1/2, #5 = 3/4, #4 = 1 oz.

### 6.2 Bait specifics

**PowerBait.** Formulations that produce here: **Mice Tails** (white head / pink tail is
the named combination), **floating trout worms** (bubblegum pink is a staple; also white
PowerBait topping a pink worm), **Power Eggs**, dough (ranks below worms/eggs among
guides). Colors named in reports: **orange scented garlic** (fall), orange and
brown/peach eggs (Oct/Nov), **sunrise/orange** (late winter), yellow or orange eggs
(mid-March), **white over pink** (summer), pink or chartreuse (bank anglers, general).
**Pattern: orange/garlic in fall and early spring; pink and white in low clear water;
chartreuse as the all-round backup.** `[REPORTED]`

**Rigging to float.** The leader length sets the height; on the standard rig a floating
bait rides roughly **6–18 in** up, and the stated local target for a Mouse Tail is *"a
foot or so off the bottom."* Three documented methods: inflate the crawler, thread a
**mini marshmallow** up the shank (Cotter Trout Dock lists miniature marshmallows and
inflated night crawlers as *the* two flotation methods on this river), or use floating
Mice Tails / Power Eggs. **Rig a Mice Tail through the center of the worm and out ~½ in
behind the head** so it floats horizontally — head-hooked it hangs vertically, which is
wrong. **Use mono, not fluoro, on the hook leg** — fluoro sinks and kills the lift.

**Night crawlers — this is the guide bait, not PowerBait.** Carl Meeks ranks red worms →
crawfish tails → night crawlers, choosing crawlers for durability on rocky bottom.
Standard: **1½-in stubs on a No. 6**. Trophy/high-water: **two whole night crawlers
threaded on a No. 6 with the tails left dangling, cast downstream from an anchored
position** — the single most directly applicable technique in this research for a boat
tied to the bank. **Inflating is explicitly a White River thing**: AGFC reports name
"pumped up nightcrawlers," "air-filled nightcrawlers drifted near bottom for brown
trout," and "a fat, floating nightcrawler" for larger browns. Method: **hook first, then
inject** — 4–5 bubbles spaced along a large crawler, smallest hook that will hold it.

**Sculpin.** *The* named trophy-brown bait, in nearly every AGFC report from this
section: *"slimy sculpins, particularly blue-bellied varieties… browns favor slimy
sculpins under rocks during low water."* 2–5 in; lips-hooked; **split shot about a foot
above**, or the standard rig. Present **in front of the openings and near the bottom of
large rocks** — that's where they live. Circle hooks preferred to reduce deep hooking;
sweep, don't swing. ⚠️ **Commercial availability is murky** — sold at some shops, but
forum anglers flag uncertainty about the legality of selling them; most people catch
their own flipping rocks. **Verify AGFC baitfish/transport rules before buying or moving
live sculpin.**

**Minnows — Cranor's documented method.** 3-in shiner on a **#6 Eagle Claw Kahle through
both lips**; 1/8 oz to three generators, 3/16 for 3–6, 1/4 for 7–8. **Cast upstream of
the target and let it drift naturally** — drift speed is the whole game. Fish behind
logs/rocks/boulders, downstream of islands, along drop-offs, and inside bends where slow
meets fast. Best December through mid-April, especially high water.

**Shrimp is the sleeper.** Named constantly: *"shrimp and egg combo performing
exceptionally well," "UV eggs tipped with shrimp," "spoons combined with shrimp or
crawdad tail for great-sized fish."* Plain cocktail shrimp, peeled, pea-sized chunks.
Survives current far better than dough.

**Beads** are the modern egg: hard plastic in **orange-matte or peach**, pegged a couple
of inches above a bare **No. 6**, on an 18-in leader. **Corn** is legal and effective but
a distant third; **do not chum with it.**

## 7. Presentation from a boat tied to the bank

### 7.1 This is the safe method, and locals say so

OzarkAnglers regulars, verbatim: **"Most of the drownings that occur on the White are
because of people throwing out an anchor during heavy generation periods."** The
recommended alternative is **tying up high to a tree** rather than anchoring in current.
Guide-boat doctrine on the White is *"never anchor in current."* `[REPORTED]`

**Safety specifics:**

- **Tie from the BOW, bow pointed upstream.** A stern- or side-tied boat in current gets
  rolled or swamped. Slip knot / quick-release at the cleat.
- **Keep a sharp fixed-blade knife in a sheath within arm's reach.** Universal river
  doctrine, repeated specifically for the White.
- **Tie HIGH and leave slack**, and re-tend the rope every 20–30 min once water is
  coming. Generation can raise the river several feet in a couple of hours; a tight, low
  rope on a rising river pulls a gunwale under.
- **Know what's downstream before committing to a spot.**

### 7.2 Where to tie

Meeks' principle: **"Put your boat in the faster water and fish in the slower water"** —
for a bank-tied boat, tie in the slack/eddy along the bank and cast to the **seam** where
slow meets fast. Cranor's target water is the same: behind logs, rocks and boulders;
downstream of islands; along drop-offs; inside bends where slow current meets fast.

Reach-specific `[INFERRED]` from §2: the head of the White Hole just below the ramp; the
weed-bed edge upstream of the ramp; both sides of the Narrows island (deep water far
side); the drop-off at the tail of any shoal; and once generation starts, the flooded
grass and gravel bars that were dry an hour before.

**Rotate every 45–60 minutes.** Untie, drop 100 yards, retie.

### 7.3 The four presentations

Because the boat can't move, **the bait has to.**

1. **45° upstream cast + natural swing.** Cast 45°+ up, sink on slack, follow with the
   rod tip as it drifts past, let it come tight below the boat and **hold**. The
   documented White River bank technique. Many takes come at the dangle.
2. **Downstream / quartering-down (Meeks' anchored technique).** Cast downstream and let
   the current do the work; the flow holds the leader straight and lifts the bait in the
   column. This is exactly how Meeks fishes two whole crawlers from an anchored boat in
   high water. Don't yank it back when it starts to rise.
3. **Walking the rig down (back-bounce / drop-back).** With the rig on bottom below the
   boat, **lift the rod tip to unweight the sinker, feed 3–6 ft of line, let it
   re-settle.** Repeat. Turns a fixed position into a 100-yard drift with no recast. Fan
   it systematically: near seam, then a rod-length out, then the far seam.
4. **Float rig — the highest-leverage adaptation.** Set depth so the bait rides **1–3 ft
   off bottom**; fixed float under 12 ft, slip float deeper. Under it: a 1/16–3/32 oz
   marabou jig, a Trout Magnet, or a worm/egg on a #10–#12 with split shot. **Cast up,
   let the float run 40–80 ft downstream on a controlled feed, reel back, repeat.** This
   is the closest thing to a genuine long drift available from a fixed boat. Local
   caveat: float-and-jig works *"when water is not moving or moving slow"* — favor it
   from min flow to 1–2 units and switch to the bottom rig above that.

### 7.4 Spinners from a fixed position

Work the clock face: **quartering upstream (10–11 o'clock) is the primary** — let it sink
to bottom first, then a steady retrieve keeping the blade thumping, and expect the take
*"as the Rooster Tail starts to swing downstream and angle across the current."* Then
straight across (rod tip at 9 o'clock, reel slightly slower than the current, follow the
lure downstream with the tip). Then **the downstream hang** — let it swing all the way
below the boat and hold it; the blade turns on flow alone with almost no retrieve. **This
is a free presentation only a stationary boat gets, and it's the highest-percentage way
to cover the seam directly below.** Then **feed line in 3–6 ft increments** to walk the
spinner progressively downstream.

**Never clip a snap swivel directly to the spinner** — it pushes the blade off balance.
Run an 18-in fluoro leader to a small barrel or ball-bearing swivel up the main line.

### 7.5 Snag avoidance

**Get the bait up** — the single biggest fix; the drop-shot geometry is inherently
snag-resistant because the bait never touches rock. **Sacrificial dropper** in lighter
line, or the rubber-band-to-three-way trick. **Bell over egg sinker in rock** (the bell's
shape kicks over stones), but a slip sinker where you want the fish to run without
feeling weight. **Don't over-weight** — an over-weighted sinker plants itself in a
crevice.

### 7.6 Etiquette

Downstream boats hold mid-river, upstream boats hug the bank. The downstream boat has
priority running a shoal unless one is already occupied. No-wake around anchored boats
and anyone standing in the water. Don't chum upstream of non-chumming boats. **"Never go
between a fisherman and the bank."** `[REPORTED]`

## 8. Fly-rod annex

Relevant only to Brian's own rod (9 ft 5-wt Orvis Recon; two Suntech Kurenai tenkara
rods, 30R and 33R).

**A tied-off boat is a fixed position, and Dally explicitly says swinging streamers
*"works best wading or from fixed positions."*** The swing is the primary method here,
not a consolation prize.

**Ranked for this exact situation `[INFERRED]`:**

1. **Tightline/high-stick the near seam.** Highest fish-per-hour and the most
   seat-friendly. Long thin leader (10–12 ft, or 5 ft of 20 lb + 3 ft of 12 lb + tippet
   ring + 3–4 ft of 4X–5X fluoro), heavy jig point fly, small dropper 18 in above. Flick
   8–15 ft upstream, lead down with the tip high. **The only thing that works well in
   wind.**
2. **Swing a soft hackle or small streamer.** Shorten to 6–7½ ft of leader. Cast across
   and slightly down, one upstream mend, let it come tight and swing. **Don't strike.**
   Hang and pulse 6–10 seconds at the dangle. Cover water by lengthening: 20, 25, 30, 35 ft.
3. **Short indicator drift.** 20–25 ft max — anything longer is un-mendable from a seat.
   Indicator-to-fly ≈ 1.5× depth. Stick-on indicators at low water (they hold on fine
   tippet where a Thingamabobber slips); Airlock/Thingamabobber with real weight.
4. **Dry-dropper.** Situational — October hoppers, April caddis. Fish it into the soft
   water the boat itself creates, where drag isn't the issue.

**Is a 5-weight enough?** Yes for ~90%. Dally names the 9-ft 5-wt as the foundational
Ozark trout rod and lists the Recon by name; Tailwaters calls a 9-ft 4–6 wt *"the
standard rod used by the vast majority of all anglers."* **The rod is not the limiting
factor — tippet and drag are.** What it genuinely can't do: throw articulated streamers
or big lead-eye flies, punch into hard wind, or comfortably deep-nymph at 3+ units.

**The cheap compromise (the shop's own recommendation):** a fast-sinking VersiLeader or
poly-leader looped onto the floating line — Dally calls it an *"insta-sink-tip"* — plus
3–4 ft of 12–16 lb fluoro and weighted #6–10 flies. **Swung, not stripped.** ~$15.

**Fly list — the 8 that cover both trips:** Sunday Special (#14, and #12 with tungsten),
gray sowbug #14–16, Ruby Midge #16–18, Zebra Midge #16–18 (black/nickel, red/nickel),
fluorescent pink San Juan worm, peach/orange egg, olive Woolly Bugger #8–10, black
Girdle Bug #8–10. **Add for April:** Tailwater Soft Hackle in caddis green #14 (the
swinging fly) and Elk Hair Caddis #14. **Add for October:** #10 hopper.

**Tenkara — the honest verdict `[REPORTED]` + `[INFERRED]`.** TenkaraBum's own specs put
the Kurenai 30 and 33 as *"ideal for trout measuring 4–10 inches, with capacity for
occasional 10–11 inch fish"* and states they are **"too light for consistent 14-inch
trout."** No warranty. The average White River rainbow is at or above that line — the
regulations are written around a 14-inch rainbow. **No published account of anyone fly
fishing tenkara on the White or Norfork exists that I could find.** The closest real data
is TenkaraBum fishing both rivers with **keiryu** rods and weighted rigs, needing **four
BB shot instead of his usual one** because of wind.

Where they can work: **true minimum flow only**, in soft shallow edges and tailouts,
backwaters and eddies, and the soft bank slot beside the tied-off boat (a 33 reaches 20+
ft from a seat with no backcast). Size 2.5 level line, 3–4 ft of 6X, **one** unweighted
or micro-bead fly, no tungsten. Hard limits: effective depth ~18 in, any real current
bellies the line instantly, wind is fatal, and the rod is rated below the average fish.
**If fixed-line is ever to be a real tool here, the answer is a keiryu rod, not another
tenkara rod.**

**Two rods in one small boat `[INFERRED]`, geometry is the whole solution:** bow
upstream; bait angler in the stern fishing straight downstream (static line, 30–60 ft
out, in the boat's own current lane); fly angler forward, casting up-and-across to the
**opposite** side. Different quadrants, never intersect. Roll cast and side-arm, or a
Belgian/oval cast — a 9-ft rod and a beaded nymph at eye level with someone sitting
behind you is a real injury risk. **A five-gallon bucket with a couple inches of water in
it** for the fly line. **One rod per angler, attended at all times** (§9) — no propping a
bait rod in a holder while fly casting.

## 9. Regulations

⚠️ **This fishery is under active emergency management and two authoritative sources
currently disagree. Verify by phone (AGFC 833-345-0325) before every trip.**

**Current limits, effective Feb 1 2026** `[CONFIRMED]` from AGFC:

- **White River, Bull Shoals Dam → Norfork Access:** keep **two rainbow trout under 14
  inches**; **all other trout species must be released immediately.** This covers the
  entire reach in §2 and makes it functionally a brown-trout catch-and-release fishery.
- Norfork Access → Hwy 58: two rainbows under 14 in, OR one under 14 plus one of
  (rainbow/brook over 14, or brown/cutthroat/tiger over 24).

⚠️ `[CONFLICT]` — the eRegulations site still publishes the **historic** rule: *"Daily
limit of 5 trout; only 1 trout over 14 inches long may be kept."* AGFC's own trout page
carries the reduced 2026 limits. **Treat AGFC as authoritative and confirm by phone.**

**Standing rules for the Bull Shoals Tailwater** (defined as 100 yd below the dam to the
Hwy 58 bridge at Guion) `[CONFIRMED]`:

- **Bait fishing: single hooking point per pole only.** No trebles with PowerBait,
  worms, shrimp, corn, eggs, sculpin, or any scented bait. Multi-point barbed hooks are
  legal on *lures* outside the C&R areas.
- **One rod or pole per angler, attended at all times.**
- **No fishing, boating or wading within 100 yards of the dam.**
- **Trout permit required** in addition to a fishing license, 16+.
- At night, statewide, **only rainbow trout may be harvested**.

**Bull Shoals Catch-and-Release Area** — 100 yd below the dam to the **upstream boundary
of Bull Shoals/White River State Park**:

- **Feb 1 – Oct 31:** all trout released. **Artificial lures or flies only, all hooking
  points barbless, no natural or scented bait, no chumming.** *Open during both trip
  windows.*
- **Nov 1 – Jan 31:** **closed to fishing and wading entirely**, and the closure extends
  downstream to the **wing dike at the state park trout dock**.
- Wing dike → downstream park boundary, Nov 1 – Jan 31: brown trout C&R, artificials,
  barbless, **no fishing 30 min after sunset to 30 min before sunrise**.

⚠️ `[CONFLICT]` on the C&R boundary: the Arkansas code says 100 yd below the dam → the
*upstream* park boundary; one outfitter describes it as Dew Eddy Shoals → the
*downstream* park boundary. **Read the posted signs on the ground.**

**Gaston's downstream — the entire §2 reach — is below the C&R zone and under general
tailwater rules.**

   almost certainly already owned; otherwise a ~$4 spool of 4 lb green mono. Highest-
   impact single change on this list.
2. **No small bait hooks.** #4 Aberdeen fishes a Mice Tail or dough ball fine, just
   oversized. #6–#8 light wire is the spec. ~$3, optional.
3. **No 1/6 oz spinner** — *the* named Rooster Tail size here. The ¼ oz flame/chartreuse
   Rooster Tail, Mepps Aglia #3 silver and Black Fury #3 are all quarter-ounce class
   (right from 2 units up); the 1/16 Beetle Spin covers the bottom. **Brackets the gap
   closely enough not to buy.**

### Leave home

Whole topwater tray (Ploppers, poppers, Hula Popper, Crazy Crawler), buzzbaits, the
spinnerbait, Johnson Silver Minnows, Senkos and wacky sticks, football jigs, wire leader.
**And the method feeders and jello/panko pack bait** — pack bait is not a White River
technique and **chumming is prohibited in the C&R areas.**

### Not in the inventory

Rods and reels. What this water wants: a 6½–7 ft light or medium-light spinning rod,
1000-size reel with a smooth drag, 4–6 lb clear or light-green mono.
